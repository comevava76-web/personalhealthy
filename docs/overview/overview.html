1. What HINT 365 is for

HINT 365 is a personal diary on an Android phone. A person records their blood pressure (the top number SYS, the bottom number DIA and the pulse PUL) and keeps the results of their lab reports. The app shows them in tables, charts and a PDF report that can be sent to a doctor.

It is not a medical device. HINT 365 does not measure anything, does not say whether a value is good or bad, does not diagnose and does not give advice. It only keeps track of what the person records. The doctor decides what the values mean. For this reason no colour on any screen means "good" or "bad", and there is no red.

Responsibility. The person checks every value before saving it and against the original report. The owner provides the service as described in the terms of use (accepted in the app at the first start and at every update) and handles personal data as described in the privacy policy. Those two documents are binding; this one only explains.

3. What the person does, step by step

Figure: Using the app: from the first start to the report for the doctor.

First time

  1. Installs the app from the download link (later from Google Play).
  2. Signs in with Google. The Google account is used only to recognise the person on a new phone. The email address is never stored on the server: only a coded fingerprint of the account that cannot be turned back into the email.
  3. Accepts the terms of use and the storage of health data. Without that the app does not open. The terms say clearly that HINT 365 is not a medical device, gives no diagnosis or advice, and that treatments and medicines are never started, stopped or changed because of the app: the doctor decides. The acceptance is recorded as proof (pseudonymous account code, coded fingerprints of the account and the phone, version, language, date; never the email) and copied into a second database in the EU as a backup. Right after accepting, the app offers to save the terms as a PDF on the phone; nothing is downloaded without the person's tap. A reading bar at the top fills while the person scrolls; the boxes and Accept work only once the text has been read to the end. The terms and the privacy policy can be opened at any time from the links at the bottom of the screen (the terms' PDF is saved in Downloads from there, never opened by itself), in the app's language (English when the phone uses a language HINT 365 does not have, for example Georgian).
  4. The phone creates its own private key, which never leaves the phone. Every request the app sends is signed with it, so the server knows it really comes from that phone.

Every day

  • Opening the app: the app uses the phone's own lock (fingerprint, face or PIN). HINT 365 has no PIN of its own. If the phone has no lock, the app suggests one, at most every 14 days; it is only a suggestion.
  • Voice (blue button): the person says the three numbers; the phone turns speech into text; the app finds SYS, DIA and PUL; the person checks them and confirms; only then are they saved.
  • Scan (violet button with a small magic wand, an AI feature): the person photographs the monitor's display; the photo goes to the server and from there to Anthropic, which reads the three numbers; the photo is not kept anywhere; the person checks the numbers and confirms. Free for 15 days (3 a day), then with the 4.99 US$ yearly subscription.
  • Lab reports: the person picks a PDF or a photo of a report; the phone reads it by itself; only the date and the values as printed (no name, no personal details) go to the server. If anything is unclear, nothing is saved.
  • Looking back: the last reading, the average of each day of the last 7 days, the PDF report in Downloads.
  • Web Dashboard: a button opens the browser with large charts and the lab table; "Send to the doctor" sends the PDF and a read-only link valid 7 days.

The app and the browser never touch the database directly: everything goes through the Worker, which checks who is asking.

What happens when the person saves a reading (the sequence of calls)

  1. The app sends POST /v1/bp/voice (or /v1/bp/photo) to the Worker, signed with the phone's key, with the app version.
  2. The Worker checks the signature, that the request is fresh and not a copy, that this app version is allowed, and that the terms were accepted.
  3. The Worker writes the three numbers in D1, linked to the person's anonymous account code, and answers "saved".
  4. For the Scan, one call comes first: POST /v1/bp/photo/read. The Worker checks the trial or the subscription and the daily limit, passes the photo to Anthropic with the owner's key, gets three numbers back and returns them to the phone. Nothing is kept.
  5. For the Web Dashboard: the app asks the Worker for a one-time code (POST /v1/web/code); the browser opens with it and gets a technical cookie valid 7 days; the page then asks the Worker for the data (/my/api/data), which reads them from D1, and draws the charts and the PDF in the browser.

The database: tables and relations

One database, Cloudflare D1 in the EU (personalhealthy), and a second one only for the backup of the acceptances (personalhealthy-backup). Only the server reads and writes them. Every personal table hangs from persons (one account): deleting the account deletes all its rows, except the acceptances of the terms, kept as proof.

6. Personal data and compliance

Figure: Compliance: each rule becomes a control, a piece of evidence and a check.

  • Laws: the EU GDPR and the Swiss data protection law (FADP). Blood pressure and lab results are health data.
  • Consent: asked explicitly before first use; withdrawn by deleting the account.
  • Pseudonymous, not anonymous: the data are linked to an account code, never to a name or an email.
  • Where: Cloudflare D1 in the EU. Anthropic (Scan photo, not kept) and Google (sign-in, payments) are in the US, under their transfer safeguards.
  • How long: blood-pressure readings at most 365 days (deleted every night); lab results until the person deletes them; error codes 90 days; acceptances of the terms kept as proof, with a backup copy in a second EU database (personalhealthy-backup), never with an email.
  • Rights: see, download, correct and delete one's data, in the app or on the web; answer within a month.
  • Checks: every control and its evidence is listed in Admin → Observability and in the dossier docs/compliance/HINT365-Compliance.pdf. It is the developer's self-assessment, not legal advice.

9. Defect management: how a problem gets fixed

Figure: Defect management: every day, from the error to the fix in a new version.

  1. Found: a user meets an error (the app and the web send its code to the error log), or a test fails, or the owner reports it in the chat.
  2. Collected: every morning the Error log report opens an issue with the last 24 hours.
  3. Analysed at 06:48 by the morning routine: is it a real bug or a normal situation (a wrong phone clock, one blurred photo)?
  4. Recorded in the problem registry with its cause (status: open).
  5. Fixed on a branch, with a test that would have caught it; all checks green; merged; released the same day.
  6. Closed: the registry says fixed, in which version and with which pull request; the issue is closed; the owner gets a short summary.
  7. Large or unclear fixes, and anything touching the terms, wait for the owner.

10. Security: vulnerability management

Figure: Vulnerability management: every Monday, from the scan to the fix or a written reason.

A vulnerability is a known weakness in a piece of software we use. New ones are published every day; we look for them once a week, on Monday, and fix them the same morning when we can.

The weekly cycle

  1. Monday 06:10 Zurich (04:10 UTC): the scan runs and updates the list.
  2. Monday 06:48: the routine takes every open finding, fixes what it can (update the library, run all tests, release the same day).
  3. For each one it cannot fix, it writes the short reason in decisions.json (shown in the list).
  4. Next Monday: the scan confirms; what was fixed is gone; what is left keeps its reason or gets a new date.
Where we are on 02.10.2026. The only findings inside the app (Bouncy Castle) were fixed in version 0.1.120. The 59 left are all build tools (brought in by the Android Gradle plugin 8.5.2): they disappear with its major update (issue #94), which waits for the owner's go. The Kotlin build plugin (issue #97) is an accepted risk until Kotlin 2.x is stable.

The "Fix" button of the Security console has been removed: every fix is made by the Monday routine at no extra cost.

11. Patch management: keeping libraries up to date

Figure: Patch management: library updates, small ones merged, large ones decided by the owner.

  • Every Monday GitHub's Dependabot opens a pull request for every library that has a newer version.
  • Small updates (patch and minor): merged by the morning routine once all checks are green, then released.
  • Large updates (major): proposed to the owner, because they can change how things work.
  • Security patches are applied on Monday, together with the weekly scan; a critical one found in the app can be fixed any day, with a release the same day.
  • After a security release every older version stops by itself: only the newest version works, and the app offers the update.

12. Change management: how a change is made

Figure: Change management: every change goes through a branch, the checks and, for binding texts, the owner.

  1. The owner asks for a change in the chat, in their own words.
  2. The developer makes it on a separate branch (claude/…), never directly on the main code.
  3. In the same change the developer updates every document it touches (this document, the guides, the terms, the architecture).
  4. A pull request is opened. GitHub runs the checks: about 180 functional and security tests, the app build, the documents check. Nothing can be merged while a check is red.
  5. Changes to the terms of use, the privacy policy or the app texts need the owner's approval on GitHub (CODEOWNERS); the owner says "go" in the chat.
  6. The change is merged into the main code. That starts the release.

13. Release management: how a new version reaches people

Three things are released: the server and the Web Dashboard (Cloudflare), the Android app (Google Play) and, later, the iPhone app (Apple App Store). The server and the web are live a few minutes after the merge and nobody has to do anything. The app needs the store's review first; only when the store has published it to everyone does the server ask for it, and then every phone updates the next time the app is opened. Times are typical, not promises: the store review is decided by Google or Apple.

  1. Merge into the main code starts the Build pipeline on GitHub.
  2. Tests again, on the exact code being released.
  3. Server: the Worker is put online on Cloudflare, the database tables are updated, the secrets are set.
  4. App: the APK is built and signed with the app's key; the version number goes up by one (0.1.121, 0.1.122…).
  5. Package saved: a GitHub Release v0.1.N keeps the APK and the AAB (the file Google Play wants) for ever.
  6. Check: the pipeline verifies that the download link serves the new version.
  7. The developer sends the link in the chat; the owner installs and tests it; the update installs over the old app, data stay.

When HINT 365 is on Google Play

  1. Steps 1–5 above are the same: every release already produces the signed AAB.
  2. The AAB is uploaded to the Play Console, first to the internal testing track (the owner and a few testers).
  3. The owner tests that version from Google Play.
  4. The owner promotes it to production, with a staged rollout (for example 20% of users, then 100% after a day without new errors).
  5. Google reviews the version (from a few hours to a few days); then phones update by themselves.
  6. Only one version is kept. The app asks Google Play at every start: when a newer version exists, Google's own update window opens inside the app (the standard "in-app update" of Google Play; the App Store equivalent on iPhone later), and the person updates without leaving the app. The server, too, accepts only the newest version: an older app cannot save anything until it is updated. After the update the new terms of use are asked, if they changed.
  7. The server asks for a new version only when Google has published it to everyone: every hour the server reads the version live on Google Play (a version still in review or rolled out to part of the people does not count). Nobody is stopped before Google can give them the update.

If something goes wrong

  • Stop the rollout in the Play Console, or put the previous verified version back online (Promote verified APK and server).
  • Block one or more faulty versions on every phone at once (Actions → App versions): the app offers only the update. Data stay.

How a recovery runs

  1. By hand (only if the Cloudflare account is new): sign in, keep the subdomain comevava76, create the API token, put it in GitHub; check the GitHub secrets (list in the runbook).
  2. Pipeline Actions → Disaster recovery → rebuild, confirm RESTORE: creates the three databases in the EU, the tables, takes last night's backup, checks its fingerprint, decrypts it, puts the data back, shows the counts, and starts Build.
  3. Pipeline Build: secrets into the server, server and Web Dashboard online, signed app, download check.
  4. By hand, only what applies: Anthropic (a new AI key and its spend limit), Google (the sign-in client, only if the address changed), Google Play (the server's access), the owner's phone (owner code).
  5. By hand, the check: health address, sign in on the app, readings and lab results there, Web Dashboard, Admin numbers, a new backup made.

Proof that it works

On the 1st of every month the pipeline runs a drill by itself: it builds a complete copy of the databases from zero, restores last night's backup into it, compares the counts with production, measures the time and deletes the copy. First test, 02.10.2026, on a local copy: rebuilt and restored in 24 seconds, same counts as the original (7 accounts, 11 readings, 5 acceptances).
